## 6. Observations supported by the data

* Credit unions are the consumer-lending part of the market: 184 of 286 classified CDFI credit unions are
  primarily consumer lenders, and the rest are mainly mortgage lenders; 286 report 12,097,351 members in total.
* CDFI banks are mostly real-estate lenders: commercial real estate is the primary focus for
  175 and mortgages for 109 of 328 classified banks/BHCs; consumer lending is a
  median 3.2% of bank loans. Regulatory small-business loans (≤$1MM) are a median
  16.8% of bank loans, but much of that is small CRE, not C&I.
* Loan funds are the business- and housing-lending part of the market: of 483 classified loan funds,
  246 are in segments B or D (micro/small business), 74 in C (homeownership),
  108 in E (housing development / real estate / facilities) and 46 in A (consumer).
  Loan funds are small: median total assets $19M.
* Native CDFIs (65) are spread across segments: C 18, A 16, U 11, D 9, E 6, B 5.

## 7. Caveats

* Evidence-based loan-fund focus is text-derived; it shows what the organization *describes*, not dollar portfolio
  shares. Expense-weighted program descriptions are the strongest signal; SDL/CMF/NMTC awards and names are weak.
* Credit-union microenterprise lending, housing development and community facilities are not observable in the 5300.
* Bank "microenterprise" is a size proxy (C&I loans with original amount ≤ $100K).
* 81 credit unions (mostly Puerto Rico cooperativas) and 16 banks/BHCs have no current regulatory data and are unclassified.
